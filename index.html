DV death review recommendations: who's acting on them?

Every state DV/family-violence death review body makes recommendations to stop the next death. This tracks what happened after — jurisdiction by jurisdiction, straight from each body's own reporting. 232 recommendations are now extracted individually for NSW, SA and WA, each with the government's own words about it and a link back to the page it came from.

What the numbers say, by jurisdiction

NSW — DVDRT
40%
Partial
Of 122 recommendations made 2011–2019, the Team assesses 49 as implemented and 10 as having had no action at all.

All 122 are now extracted individually. Read against the government's own responses rather than its summary table, the picture is close: 45 implemented, 12 never started or refused. Two independent measurements landing in the same place.

And two were refused, then done anyway. Recommendation 1.1 was refused in 2018 — the government "does not intend to become a member of Our Watch" — and its 2019 update reads "Complete. In July 2019, the NSW Government joined Our Watch." Published proof that "not supported" is a position, not an outcome.

The most recent government word on any NSW recommendation is from 2021: the 2021–23 report publishes no recommendations and no status chapter at all.
Sources: DVDRT Report 2019–2021, Table 7.2 for the Team's own assessment; the 2015–17, 2017–19 and 2019–21 response chapters for the per-recommendation figures. These are two different measurements of the same 122 recommendations and are not summed. "Implemented" means the agency said it did the thing, not that anyone verified it.
QLD — DFVDRAB
84%
Mostly actioned
Of 80 recommendations since 2016, 67 are implemented and 7 more are in progress. Only 1 was ever rejected (noted) by government.
Source: DFVDRAB Annual Report 2024–25, p.19
WA — Ombudsman
9/9
Claimed, not verified
All 9 recommendations from the 2022 investigation had "steps taken or proposed" per the 2023 follow-up — agency self-report, not confirmed completion.

All 72 WA recommendations are now extracted: 54 from the 2015 violence restraining orders investigation, 9 from 2022, and 9 from the 2026 review of 237 fatalities. The 9/9 figure above covers only the 2022 set, which is the only one with a published follow-up. None of the 72 is a restatement of another — WA's reports are discrete investigations rather than an annual cycle, so there is no mechanism by which a recommendation would be formally re-made.
Source: 2023 implementation report

SA — State Coroner
38
Extracted here; not tracked by SA
SA publishes no tally, so we built one: 38 recommendations pulled inquest-by-inquest out of general State Coroner annual reports, which is where DV recommendations are buried. Two are repeats — in one, the Coroner re-made an earlier recommendation word for word and said so.

And SA appears to have stopped. Across the 17 reports in the series, the word "homicide" appears zero times in every report from 2018–19 onward, while "coroner" appears 76–129 times in those same files — so that is the reports' content, not a failed text extraction. The last report with substantial DV content was 2017–18.
Source: State Coroner annual reports, all 17 extracted. Covers the annual reports only — SA publishes individual inquest findings separately and those were not examined, so this is not a claim that SA reviews nothing.

These numbers aren't directly comparable to each other — different time windows, different self-reporting standards, different definitions of "implemented." Each tile stands on its own source, not on a shared scale. That unevenness is itself part of the story.

Jurisdictions with no comparable tracking

ACT
Legislated, nothing published
Corrected 4 Nov 2026. This previously read "no mechanism exists", sourced to the Australian Human Rights Commission's 2016 call to expand DV death review — a ten-year-old document being cited as current. An ACT Family Violence Death Review was established through legislation in 2021 and the formal process is still being developed. So the gap is not the absence of a body; it is that there is no published recommendation register to track.
Source: AIJA National DFV Bench Book, death review, updated August 2025
TAS
No mechanism
The one jurisdiction with no formal death review process at all — not "the same gap as the ACT", as this previously said. Family violence deaths are examined only by a coroner, and those investigations are often not released publicly. A review of the Coroners Act was underway as of July 2026.
Source: AIJA National DFV Bench Book, death review, updated August 2025
NT
Ad hoc only
Correction to an earlier assumption in this project: the NT is not a "nothing" jurisdiction like the ACT/TAS — it has no standing review body, but individual coronial inquests do make recommendations. The November 2024 inquest into the deaths of 4 Aboriginal women made 35 recommendations; the NT Government's July 2025 response claims 24 of 35 are "already in place" — a self-reported claim, not independently verified, and not something this tracker can confirm without the underlying evidence.
VIC
Discontinued
The Family Violence Reform Implementation Monitor tracked all 227 Royal Commission recommendations annually — the most thorough mechanism of any state — but the office closed 31 December 2022. Monitoring moved inside the Department of Premier & Cabinet and is no longer independent or published on the same annual cycle.
