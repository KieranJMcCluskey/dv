DV death review recommendations: who's acting on them?

Australian governments have been told 232 times what would stop the next domestic violence death. 53 of those recommendations are confirmed implemented. 22 were never started or were refused outright. The rest sit in progress or have no status at all. Every one is listed below, by state, with the status its own government recorded against it.

What the numbers say, by jurisdiction

NSW DVDRT
40%
49 of 122 implemented, on the Team's own assessment
Partial
122 recommendations, 2011 to 2019. The Team records 49 as implemented and 10 as never acted on. Read against the government's own responses, the figures land in the same place: 45 implemented, 12 never started or refused.

Two were refused, then done anyway. On recommendation 1.1 the government said in 2018 it "does not intend to become a member of Our Watch". Its 2019 update on the same recommendation: "Complete. In July 2019, the NSW Government joined Our Watch." A refusal is a position, not an outcome.

Nobody has reported since 2021. The 2021–23 report contains no recommendations and no status chapter.
Sources: DVDRT Report 2019–2021, Table 7.2 for the Team's own assessment; the 2015–17, 2017–19 and 2019–21 response chapters for the per-recommendation figures. These are two different measurements of the same 122 recommendations and are not summed. "Implemented" means the agency said it did the thing, not that anyone verified it.
QLD DFVDRAB
84%
67 of 80 implemented, on the Board's own table
Mostly actioned
Of 80 recommendations since 2016, 67 are implemented and 7 more are in progress. Only 1 was ever rejected (noted) by government.
Source: DFVDRAB Annual Report 2024–25, p.19
WA Ombudsman
0%
0 of 72 confirmed implemented
None confirmed
Not one of the 72 is confirmed implemented. The closest thing to progress is the 2023 follow-up, which records "steps taken or proposed" against the 9 recommendations from the 2022 investigation. That is the agency reporting on itself.

72 WA recommendations are listed: 54 from the 2015 violence restraining orders investigation, 9 from 2022, 9 from the 2026 review of 237 fatalities. Only that 2022 set has a published follow-up at all. The other 63 recommendations have none.
Source: 2023 implementation report

SA State Coroner
21%
8 of 38 implemented, extracted from inquest reports
Extracted here; not tracked by SA
SA publishes no tally, so this is the first one: 38 recommendations, taken inquest by inquest out of the general State Coroner annual reports where they sit. In one of them the Coroner re-made an earlier recommendation word for word and said so.

SA stopped reporting after 2017–18. The word "homicide" appears zero times in every annual report from 2018–19 onward. "Coroner" appears 76 to 129 times in those same files, so this is what the reports contain, not a failed text extraction.
Source: State Coroner annual reports, all 17 extracted. Covers the annual reports only. SA publishes individual inquest findings separately and those were not examined, so this is not a claim that SA reviews nothing.

These figures are not comparable with each other. The time windows differ, the self-reporting standards differ, and each jurisdiction defines "implemented" its own way. Each column rests on its own source. That unevenness is the story.

Jurisdictions with no comparable tracking

ACT
Legislated, nothing published
Legislation in 2021 established an ACT Family Violence Death Review. The formal process is still being developed. A body exists. There is no published recommendation register to track.
Source: AIJA National DFV Bench Book, death review, updated August 2025
TAS
No mechanism
The one jurisdiction with no formal death review process. A coroner examines family violence deaths, and those investigations are often not released publicly. A review of the Coroners Act was underway as of July 2026.
Source: AIJA National DFV Bench Book, death review, updated August 2025
NT
Ad hoc only
The NT has no standing review body, but individual coronial inquests do make recommendations. The November 2024 inquest into the deaths of 4 Aboriginal women made 35 recommendations; the NT Government's July 2025 response claims 24 of 35 are "already in place". That claim is self-reported, not independently verified, and this tracker cannot confirm it without the underlying evidence.
VIC
Discontinued
The Family Violence Reform Implementation Monitor tracked all 227 Royal Commission recommendations annually, the most thorough mechanism of any state. The office closed on 31 December 2022. Monitoring moved inside the Department of Premier & Cabinet and is no longer independent or published on the same annual cycle.
